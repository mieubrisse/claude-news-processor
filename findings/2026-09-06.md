Suggested Improvements
----------------------

### 1. Snapshot evals for the probabilistic guards — Guidance-Edit Gate judge and the verifier fleet

- **What:** Build eval corpora for the judge hook (`claude/hooks/cc-guidance-quality-judge/`) and the content-verifier fleet (`/writing-proofreader`, `/vr-content-*` verifiers), under the exobrain-o7k verification-machinery program.
- **Why:** Your deterministic hooks have a check-loop (the workspace-mcp denylist has its monthly audit); your *probabilistic* guards have nothing — no way to know the judge still blocks what it should after a doctrine edit or model-class change. The anatomy guide's eval doctrine is directly transplantable, and it explicitly rejects the expensive alternative: "Two non-deterministic systems interacting need larger samples, cost more per trial, are harder to judge, and produce failures that are hard to attribute." Instead: "creating an eval case means constructing the test state, appending the test user message, and letting the agent run from there."
- **How:** The mechanics that matter: (a) 50-100 cases per flow, difficulty varied; (b) cases must "encode preconditions of failure, not just happy paths" — a case starting from a clean state "passes on every config and provides no meaningful data"; (c) grade the *outcome* — "the final state and the rendered response, including the arguments of the last write" — not the path taken. For the judge: past gated edits are already constructed test states sitting in mission history — a corpus of (file state, proposed edit, block/pass verdict) triples exists for free; backtest the judge against it whenever its doctrine or model changes. For `/writing-proofreader`: cases are drafts with planted antipatterns from `/writing-antipatterns`, graded on catch/miss.
- **Impact:** high — this is the missing check-loop for every probabilistic guard, and the concrete shape exobrain-o7k has been waiting for.
- **Confidence:** likely.

### 2. Typed-record memory architecture — the candidate the auto-memory ban's retest clause named

- **What:** Not a change now. When exobrain-o7k work starts, use this post's memory architecture as the reference design for quality-checkable automated memory. Worth a note on that bead today.
- **Why:** The CLAUDE.md auto-memory prohibition names its own retirement path: "revisit when the autonomy program's verification machinery (exobrain-o7k) can quality-check auto-created memories." This post supplies the first concrete, evaluated architecture matching that condition — and its structure is what makes quality-checking *possible*: facts as "a small typed record: a key (such as shoe_size, default_store, preferred_report_cadence), a short value, a category, and the session it came from." Typed records are auditable and dedupable in a way freeform MEMORY.md accretion never is, and the source-session field is the Provenance Principle built in at the schema level.
- **How:** Three mechanics to carry over: (a) extraction runs *outside* user-facing turns — "an agent in a separate thread or process reads the conversation and creates, updates, or deletes facts in the store" (worth +13% fact recall on their internal eval, and it's exactly your existing cron-loop shape — a `/daily-state-summary`-style extractor writing typed records instead of prose); (b) three read layers — a small always-in-context set, per-turn pre-fetch "from the same signals that pre-load a skill", everything else behind a lookup tool — which is your exoneuron Goldilocks-firing problem stated as architecture; (c) the eval suite from Suggestion 1 is what quality-checks the extractor.
- **Impact:** medium-high — unblocks the one standing prohibition Kevin has said he's softening on, on his own stated terms.
- **Confidence:** likely.

### 3. Cache-segment audit of AgenC's per-mission context assembly

- **What:** Audit what `agenc prime` (SessionStart hook) and AgenC's injected context put *early* in mission context, checking one thing: does anything volatile appear before stable content?
- **Why:** Caching is prefix-based — requests read from cache "up to the first byte that differs from a previous request," and "a timestamp or the current page at the top of the system prompt... silently breaks the cache on every request." Cached reads cost a tenth of fresh ones; commerce deployments hit 90-99% by ordering content global → session → volatile. Every AgenC mission re-pays this on every turn, across the whole cron fleet, so a single misplaced timestamp early in the prime output taxes everything silently.
- **How:** The actionable unit is one question: in the assembled mission context (prime output, op-settings injections, CLAUDE.md stack), is there a date, timestamp, or per-invocation value positioned before the large stable blocks? If yes, move it to the end. Rough check: dump a mission's first-request context, diff two spawns of the same mission type, and look at where the first differing byte lands.
- **Impact:** medium — pure cost/latency, no behavior change.
- **Confidence:** likely (the mechanism is certain; whether AgenC currently violates it is the thing the audit finds out).
