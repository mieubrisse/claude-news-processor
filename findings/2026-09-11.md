----------------------

### 1. Decide on Fable 5.1 — your settings pin Opus 5, and the economics moved

- **What:** `~/.claude/settings.json` line 690 sets `"model": "opus[1m]"`, and 24 further entries hard-pin `"model": "claude-opus-5"` (every Guidance-Edit Gate judge hook). Decide deliberately whether those stay, rather than letting the pin persist by default.
- **Why:** Fable 5.1 (API id `claude-fable-5-1`) released Sep 1 at $10/$50 per million input/output — and, the number that matters for your setup, **cache reads dropped 75% to $0.25 per million**. Your cost profile is dominated by repeated context: a global CLAUDE.md of this size, a 100+ skill library, and a cron fleet re-reading both on every invocation. That is close to a pure cache-read workload, and the price of exactly that just fell by three quarters. Anthropic quotes "around 25%" total cost reduction on typical workloads and "up to around 45%" on complex agentic ones. Capability moved too, on the agentic benchmarks that describe what you actually run: Terminal-Bench 4.0 55.8% vs 42.0%, AutomationBench 31.4% vs 17.1%, OSWorld 2.0 strict 41.7% vs 36.1%. Separately, Claude Code sessions see "an average of around 60% fewer interventions per session from our cyber safeguards."
- **How:** This is a Type 2, reversible decision — one line, revertible in seconds — so the honest recommendation is to try it rather than deliberate. `/claude-api migrate` is installed in your CLI (verified in 2.1.236, alongside `prompt-audit`, `upgrade`, and `managed-agents-onboard`) and exists to validate prompts against a new model rather than assuming they carry over. The 24 judge-hook pins are a separate decision from the session default and should be made separately — see Suggestion 2.
- **Caveat, stated plainly:** I have not run a single token through Fable 5.1. Every capability number above is Anthropic's own benchmark claim, and your workload — long-horizon autonomous config work under a dense CLAUDE.md — is not what any of those benchmarks measure. The cache-read price is a fact; the capability improvement is a vendor claim.
- **Impact:** high. **Confidence:** certain on the pricing and availability, likely on whether switching improves your day-to-day.

### 2. Sweep effort levels — you run `xhigh` globally, and the curve flattens well below it

- **What:** `settings.json` line 1218 sets `"effortLevel": "xhigh"` for every session. Measure whether that setting earns its cost, starting with the 24 Guidance-Edit Gate judge hooks, which are the cleanest test case you have.
- **Why:** The cost post's finding is that effort has sharply diminishing returns — beyond medium, max effort buys "about half a point for 46% more cost" on Humanity's Last Exam — and that the better move is usually a stronger model at lower effort: "Claude Fable 5.1 at low effort matches the performance of Fable 5 at high effort at a third of the cost." Anthropic's own default for Fable 5.1 is High in Claude Code, not max. You are paying a global surcharge on every session, including the many that are mechanical. The judge hooks are where this is most measurable: each is a bounded pass/block classification, each pinned to `claude-opus-5` with `timeout: 600`, and your CLAUDE.md already records the felt cost — "roughly 15-90s per gated edit," on every guidance edit you make.
- **How:** The prerequisite is the recovered 09-06 file's Suggestion 1 — an eval corpus for the judge. You cannot safely re-tier a probabilistic guard you cannot backtest, and once you can, this becomes a measurement rather than a gamble. The corpus is nearly free: every past gated edit is an already-constructed test state, a `(file state, proposed edit, block/pass verdict)` triple sitting in mission history. Build that, then sweep the judge across model and effort tiers and keep the cheapest configuration that reproduces the historical verdicts. The global `xhigh` is the second sweep, and a coarser one — worth splitting by session type rather than answering globally.
- **Impact:** medium-high — latency and cost on an interaction you hit constantly, with no capability loss if the backtest holds. **Confidence:** likely.

### 4. The prompt anti-pattern catalog as an audit lens — with a gate, not a mandate

- **What:** Read the post's six anti-patterns as a diagnostic lens over your CLAUDE.md and skill library. Do not act on it yet.
- **Why:** The catalog describes constructions that were correct for older models and now cost accuracy on frontier ones: verification rituals ("double-check your work," "verify twice before responding"), thoroughness and emphasis boosters ("Be maximally thorough," "CRITICAL: YOU MUST ALWAYS…"), mandatory procedures and scratchpad scaffolds, stale few-shot examples, contradictory rules, and dated configuration such as manual thinking budgets. Removing them tested at 5.3% better accuracy for 14.6% lower cost. Your configuration contains all six categories in volume, deliberately.
- **The reason this is a lens and not a task:** your Patch Register states that entries are armored *because softer versions failed*, and that patches retire only through the register with the reason recorded. P-1's remove-then-restore history is the documented cost of silent softening. An audit that recommends stripping MUSTs would be recommending you re-run an experiment you have already run and lost. So the gate is the same one as Suggestion 2: de-armoring is safe only where it can be backtested. The overlap between the two is not a coincidence — both are blocked on the same missing machinery (exobrain-o7k), which is now the load-bearing dependency for three separate improvements across two digests.
- **How:** Where it is cheap and safe today: contradictory rules and stale examples are strict defects under anyone's doctrine, and finding those needs no eval corpus. `/claude-api prompt-audit` is installed and runnable — **but honestly, it will not help here.** Its scope is prompts inside API-calling application code, not `CLAUDE.md`/`SKILL.md` guidance files; the skill's own description is "Build and debug apps that use the Claude API." The catalog transfers; the tool does not. A guidance-file equivalent — the anti-pattern list added to `/prompt-engineer` doctrine, so the Guidance-Edit Gate judge applies it prospectively to new edits rather than retroactively to old ones — is the version that fits your setup, and it sidesteps the Patch Register problem entirely by governing what gets written next instead of rewriting what already works.
- **Impact:** medium. **Confidence:** likely on the catalog's validity, speculative on how much of your configuration it should actually touch.
